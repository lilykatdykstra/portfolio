Lamp-mania

Overview

Lamp-mania is a fabrication exploration series — a collection of lamp designs built to investigate how different materials and construction methods affect the character of light. Each lamp in the series was built with intentionally different constraints: a different primary material, a different diffusion strategy, and a different structural approach.

The project was as much about process and material discovery as it was about creating finished objects. Every iteration asked: what does this material do to light? What new forms does this technique make possible?

Concept & Motivation

Light is one of the most powerful tools in both interior design and experience design, yet it's often treated as an afterthought. I wanted to spend focused time understanding light at a material level — how frosted acrylic diffuses versus clear, how wood grain casts shadow patterns, how fabric softens and warms a source.

The series format gave permission to fail fast. Not every lamp in the series is a refined finished product — some are intentional experiments that exist to answer a specific question and inform the next iteration.

The Series

Lamp 01 — Frosted Acrylic Column

A tall column form built from bent and welded frosted acrylic sheet. The goal was even, glowing diffusion — the frosted surface eliminates hotspots entirely and gives the lamp an almost lunar quality. The base is weighted steel plate with a brushed finish.

Lamp 02 — Lathe-Turned Wood Shade

A wood-turned shade using walnut, turned progressively thin at the top to allow warm light to glow through the grain. The shadow pattern cast on the wall shifts depending on the angle of view. This lamp taught me the most about how material thickness directly controls light character.

Lamp 03 — Geometric Wire Frame

A bare wire frame lamp with no diffusion — the naked bulb and wire geometry create sharp, intersecting shadows that become as much a part of the piece as the object itself. Constructed from 14-gauge steel wire bent and welded at joints.

Lamp 04 — 3D Printed Voronoi Shade

A lamp shade 3D printed in translucent PLA using a voronoi lattice pattern. The irregular organic geometry creates a lace-like shadow pattern with warm color where the walls are thickest. Print settings (infill, wall thickness) were iterated four times to achieve the right balance of structural integrity and light transmission.

Process & Fabrication

Each lamp required a different set of tools and techniques — the series was deliberately designed to push into unfamiliar territory with each iteration:

  • Acrylic bending and solvent welding (Lamp 01)
  • Wood lathe turning and finishing (Lamp 02)
  • Steel wire bending, cutting, and MIG welding (Lamp 03)
  • 3D modeling in Rhino + Grasshopper, FDM printing in translucent PLA (Lamp 04)
  • All lamps wired with standard lamp cord kits and UL-listed bulb sockets

Outcome & Result

The Lamp-mania series produced four functional, finished lighting objects and a much richer understanding of how material choice drives design character. Lamp 02 (wood) and Lamp 04 (3D printed voronoi) became the strongest pieces — both are now in active use.

The series also clarified what I find most interesting about fabrication: the moment when a material starts behaving in ways you didn't predict, and you have to decide whether to fight it or follow it. The best outcomes came from following it.
