Ted's Tzatziki — Client Website

Overview

Ted's Tzatziki is a family-owned Greek tzatziki sauce brand based in Des Moines, Iowa — rooted in a story that starts in Sparta, Greece in 1935 and spans over 50 years of recipe refinement. I was brought on to design and build a full client website from scratch: brand-story-forward, easy for a non-technical client to manage, and built to grow as the business scales into wholesale distribution.

Client Brief & Goals

Ted Velman needed a website that could do a few things at once: tell the brand story, showcase products, display retailer locations, and lay the groundwork for future online ordering once shipping logistics were resolved. The priority was brand identity over e-commerce — this wasn't a store launch, it was a brand introduction.

Goal 1

Brand Storytelling

Turn a 90-year family history into a compelling narrative that builds trust with wholesale buyers and retail customers.

Goal 2

Client Autonomy

Give a non-technical client the ability to update content — products, story, recipes — without touching code.

Goal 3

Future-Ready

Build the infrastructure for online ordering (Stripe) even before shipping logistics were resolved, so the site could scale without a rebuild.

Stack & Why

Next.js (App Router)

Chose Next.js for its performance, routing flexibility, and seamless Vercel deployment. App Router gave clean page structure for a multi-section brand site.

Sanity CMS

The client autonomy requirement drove the CMS choice entirely. Sanity's embedded studio at /studio gave Ted a clean interface to edit products, the brand story, and recipes — no developer needed after handoff.

Stripe

Integrated Stripe for future payment processing even though online ordering wasn't live at launch — the infrastructure was in place so the site could scale without a rebuild when shipping was resolved.

Vercel + GoDaddy

Deployed on Vercel for automatic redeployment on every git push. Connected the GoDaddy domain tedstzatziki.com via A record and CNAME.

Design: Personality-Forward

The design was inspired by brands like Graza and Bitchin' Sauce — playful, story-driven, with strong visual personality rather than a generic e-commerce template. Key design decisions included a marquee ticker strip, wavy section dividers, hover animations, decorative cucumber accents, and alternating section colors in cream, sage green, and terracotta.

Turning a Timeline Into a Story

One of the most meaningful parts of the project was rewriting the client's family history — originally a bullet-point timeline — into a narrative. Born in the foothills of Sparta, Greece in 1935, Ted Velman grew up on a family farm where cucumbers were the largest crop and his grandmother's tzatziki recipe was always being quietly refined. That story became the emotional core of the site.

Client Handoff

After launch, Ted could log into /studio and update products, edit the brand story, add recipes, and manage retailer locations — with a CMS schema scoped around his actual workflow, not a generic template. The goal was that he'd never need to open a code editor or reach out for routine content changes.

Result

A live, deployed client website at tedstzatziki.com — brand-story-forward, fully manageable by a non-technical client, and built with the infrastructure to support online ordering when the business is ready. The project covered the full lifecycle of freelance web development: client discovery, content strategy, design, full-stack development, CMS configuration, deployment, domain setup, and client handoff.

🛠️ Full stack — Next.js, Sanity, Stripe, Vercel
🌐 Live at tedstzatziki.com
📦 CMS-enabled for client self-management
🎨 Brand-story-forward design inspired by Graza

Reflections

Working with a real client added constraints that school projects don't — unclear shipping logistics, content that arrived in pieces, and a scope that needed to serve the business's current reality while leaving room to grow. Learning to scope a CMS around a non-technical user's workflow, not just what was technically easiest, was one of the most valuable parts of the project.
