Wood + Acrylic Shelf

Overview

A functional furniture piece inspired by the work of Japanese designer Shiro Kuramata, whose career was defined by exploring transparency, weightlessness, and the poetic potential of industrial materials. This shelf translates those ideas into a hand-built object using solid wood joinery and precision-welded acrylic panels.

The result is a shelf that appears to float — the clear acrylic sections dissolve into the surrounding space while the warm wood grain grounds it. It's a study in material contrast: rigid versus flexible, opaque versus transparent, warm versus cold.

Concept & Inspiration

Kuramata's "How High the Moon" armchair (1986) and his glass furniture series were the starting point — the idea that furniture could be nearly invisible, present only as a suggestion of form. I wanted to build something that felt structural and permanent but also light enough to question whether it was really there.

The design went through several sketch iterations before landing on a format that balanced the visual interest of the acrylic panels with the honest warmth of the wood. The proportions were driven by the golden ratio to create a naturally pleasing visual weight distribution.

Fabrication Process

The wood components were cut and shaped in the woodshop using a table saw, band saw, and router table. Joinery uses mortise-and-tenon construction for the main frame — no visible hardware on the exterior face.

The acrylic panels were scored, snapped, and then solvent-welded using methylene chloride. The weld joints were polished with progressively finer grit sandpaper (up to 2000 grit) and then buffed to optical clarity. Fitting the acrylic into the wood frame required precise tolerance work — the channel grooves were routed to 0.5mm clearance.

  • Table saw and band saw for wood component cuts
  • Router table for channel grooves and edge profiles
  • Mortise-and-tenon joinery for structural frame
  • Solvent welding (methylene chloride) for acrylic panels
  • Progressive sanding to 2000 grit + polishing compound for acrylic clarity

Outcome & Result

The finished shelf successfully achieves the intended visual effect — the acrylic panels read as almost invisible, creating the illusion that the wooden shelves are floating without support. The piece is structurally sound and load-bearing, capable of holding books and objects without flex.

This project deepened my understanding of precision fabrication, material behavior (particularly how acrylic moves and stresses under temperature change), and the relationship between design concept and physical execution.
